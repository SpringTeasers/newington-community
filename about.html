About Newington

A Hartford County town of 30,536 people, incorporated in 1871, eight miles south of Hartford.

Overview

Newington is a town in Hartford County, Connecticut, in the Capitol Planning Region. It has an area of 13.1 square miles and a population of 30,536 as of the 2020 U.S. Census. The town sits eight miles south of Hartford and is served by ZIP Code 06111 and area codes 860 and 959.

The Berlin Turnpike, State Route 15, is the town's main commercial corridor and carries most of its retail and dining. Away from the turnpike the town is largely residential, with the town center and the Lucy Robbins Welles Library on Garfield Street, and Mill Pond Falls, the waterfall at the center of town that gives the fall Waterfall Festival its name.

Newington is also a working town in an unshowy way: the Connecticut Department of Transportation headquarters and the American Radio Relay League, whose station is W1AW, are both here. The town's seal carries the motto "growth and progress".

History

  1. 1636

    Settlers from the Massachusetts Bay Colony, led by Thomas Hooker, pass through the area on their way to found Hartford.

  2. 1640

    The area is first settled by English colonists as part of Wethersfield.

  3. 1721

    The parish of Newington is established within Wethersfield.

  4. 1871

    Newington is incorporated as a separate town.

  5. 1900s

    The Berlin Turnpike develops into the town's principal commercial corridor.

  6. Today

    Newington is a Hartford County town of 30,536 people (2020 U.S. Census), governed under the council-manager form.

Geography & setting

Newington covers 13.1 square miles in Hartford County, Connecticut, in the Capitol Planning Region. It is eight miles south of Hartford and borders New Britain, Farmington, Wethersfield, Rocky Hill and Berlin. The Berlin Turnpike, State Route 15, runs along the town's eastern side and is the main retail corridor; the town center sits to its west, around Garfield Street.

The town's best-known natural feature is Mill Pond Falls, the waterfall at Mill Pond in the center of town. The town also holds parks and open space, including Mill Pond Park and the Cedar Mountain hiking trail, part of the Newington Greenway system.

Community & civic life

Newington's public schools are the Newington Public Schools district (npsct.org (opens in a new tab)); the town site lists the schools at newingtonct.gov (opens in a new tab). The public library is the Lucy Robbins Welles Library at 100 Garfield Street, Newington, CT 06111, phone 860-665-8700.

The town runs parks, fields and recreation programs, and the Newington Senior & Disabled Center at 120 Cedar Street, phone 860-665-8778, serves older residents. Community life runs through the town's parks and library programs, its youth sports, and arts organizations such as the Newington Children's Theatre Company, alongside the town's volunteer and faith communities.

Town government operates from Town Hall at 200 Garfield Street, Newington, CT 06111, phone 860-665-8500. Newington uses the council-manager form of government: the Town Council appoints a professional Town Manager to run day-to-day operations, and the Mayor, currently Jon Trister (D), is a member of the Council. The Town Manager's Office is listed on the town site at newingtonct.gov (opens in a new tab). This site does not name an individual Town Manager.
